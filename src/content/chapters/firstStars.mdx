The first stars form from the only ingredients available: hydrogen, helium and a trace of lithium. Without heavier elements to help it cool, the gas stays warm and collapses into very large stars.

Exact dates are uncertain: the first stars probably ignite somewhere between 100 and 250 million years after the Big Bang. The scene compresses a star's few-million-year life so you can see the whole cycle.
