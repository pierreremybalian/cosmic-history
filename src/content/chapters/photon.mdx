After the first nuclei, the universe settles into a long, slow era ruled by light. Photons outnumber nuclei by more than a billion to one, and they keep the gas hot, ionised and opaque.

This chapter runs on a logarithmic clock, so the final clearing comes right at the end. What you see then is real data: the Planck satellite's map of the light released at that moment.
