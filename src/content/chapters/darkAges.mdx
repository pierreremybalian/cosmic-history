Once atoms form, the light from the Big Bang streams away and slowly fades. For the next 150 million years or so, there are no stars at all.

Darkness doesn't mean nothing is happening. Gravity is at work, and dark matter, which outweighs ordinary matter about five to one, gathers into the structure that galaxies will later fill.
